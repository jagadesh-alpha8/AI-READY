## What this assessment would measure

The current framework measures demonstrated institutional AI maturity. A NAAC-first product can
also measure enabling readiness: curriculum, people, computing, governance and records that
make adoption feasible. These are different findings. Report them separately; a general IT
budget must never be described as demonstrated AI adoption or AI-specific assurance.

Proposed report: (1) enabling readiness, (2) demonstrated AI activity/maturity, and (3) unresolved
evidence, with scope and period. Do not blend the two scores or reuse Silver/Gold/Platinum
thresholds until weights and tier meanings are reviewed. Keep the existing engine unchanged
while collecting a repaired full-corpus baseline. Old runs retain their frozen methodology.

## Evidence rules

- F means evidenced starting capacity/activity, A means repeated use, O means measured outcomes.
- Count a curriculum as curriculum evidence, attendance as participation and equipment as available
  capacity. None alone proves competence, actual use, sufficient capacity or improved outcomes.
- Use programme, department or deployment scope actually established by the source. Report its
  institutional coverage separately. A departmental example never becomes universal access.
- Remove the blanket rule demanding all organisational units for every adoption condition. Set
  the appropriate denominator for each indicator: students, faculty, courses, systems or projects.
- Keep scope and period checks. A currently effective 2024 regulation may support a 2025–26
  curriculum finding; a 2024 activity count does not establish 2025–26 participation. Historical
  material can support a historical baseline, labelled with its period.
- For outcomes, require two comparable completed measurement/review periods where repeated
  improvement is claimed, rather than imposing two academic years on every condition. Retain
  period definitions, methods and limitations; one-off outcomes can be described without awarding
  sustained-outcome status.
- Silence stays unknown. A failed retrieval is a processing limitation, not institutional absence.
- Foundation pairs remain substantive conditions, not a requirement to upload documents with
  particular titles. Accept equivalent evidence across multiple cited sources.

foundation pair only. Every adoption/outcome condition still requires actual AI-specific use/results.
Availability is a hypothesis about document types, not a measured prevalence across colleges.

## How to validate without reading every PDF in chat

1. First establish one full, non-colliding import. Count discovered/imported/current/readable/frozen
   documents. Do not classify missing evidence before this passes.
2. Extract text locally once; preserve document ID, source path, page and text hash. Mark OCR failures.
3. Build a local full-text index; search both rubric terms and domain synonyms, including AI, artificial
   intelligence, ML, machine learning, neural networks, named AI courses and project/service names.
   Keyword absence is a triage result, never proof of absence. Scans require OCR.
4. Inspect candidate pages and neighbouring pages only. Deduplicate copies, retain provenance, and
   separate no candidate / unreadable / historical / wrong scope / partial / sufficient evidence.
5. Start with C3.1, C4.1, C5.2, C6.1, C6.4 and C7.2; these have plausible source types in this collection.
   Include C2.3 and C6.3 as negative controls for specialised operational requirements.
6. Use the same frozen evidence for the current rubric and this proposal. Explain changed decisions
   condition by condition. Do not claim the new method is better just because its scores are higher.
